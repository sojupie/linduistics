# E0 run 001: baseline coverage and saved review cases

Date: 2026-10-03. Status: running; E0 is not complete. Coverage and development display review have run; open-vocabulary and hard-case review remain.

## What ran

The frozen five-language file was checked against its recorded SHA-256, then evaluated against all 1,712 between-language form comparisons in the selected modern IE-CoR varieties. Expected words, concepts, and expert cognate sets came from the published reference before reading pipeline predictions. Both directions count as one comparison.

The audit also saved a seeded sample of five rows per language-pair/category, with inclusion probabilities and separate blinded evidence and prediction files. Independent subtitle token-frequency samples were frozen before checking dictionary coverage.

No database writes or dataset changes occurred.

### Recorded inflection mapping

The follow-up mapping check recovered **4 of the 58 absent-exact-headword cases** through the recorded German plural `Haare` → `Haar`. Those four lemma comparisons already exist. Their concept senses still need review; inflection mapping does not establish a correct displayed meaning.

The remaining 54 cases involve 17 distinct unmapped reference expressions. Most are phrases such as “be scared,” “be sick,” or “i går,” rather than single missing words. Norwegian reference forms also include explicit notation such as `nese (nase)` and `røyk (røk)`. English `belly-button` is hyphenated, and Norwegian `hovne` has no recorded mapping in this check.

This changes the diagnosis: **58 absent exact headwords must not be described as 58 missing ordinary words.** We need separate results for multiword expressions, source notation, recorded inflections, and absent lemmas. The 213 missing comparisons with both exact headwords present remain a distinct measured gap. Original exact-spelling results are preserved in the table above.

## Decision so far

### Development display review

The source-guided agent review examined all **897 distinct displayed definitions** used by the **995 found development concept comparisons**. Classifier labels and numeric scores were hidden during review. Expected concepts came from published IE-CoR/Concepticon definitions; ambiguity checks also used IE-CoR's disambiguation notes.

| Pair outcome | Comparisons | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Both displayed definitions match the intended concept | 630 | 63.3% |
| At least one definition describes another sense or object | 259 | 26.0% |
| No clear wrong sense, but at least one definition is restricted | 99 | 9.9% |
| Unresolved wording or source identity | 7 | 0.7% |

The strict acceptance rate has a **58.2%–68.3% family-bootstrap interval**, using 2,000 resamples of 104 represented family groups. That interval describes sensitivity to the evaluated families. It does not measure reviewer correctness or establish accuracy across the whole dictionary.

These are **agent judgments checked against a published concept reference**, not newly collected independent human annotations. The expected concepts and expert cognate sets have an independent published source; the judgment that a displayed gloss matches that concept was made by the agent. Reviewer disagreement and human agreement cannot be measured from this single review pass.

The check is **conditioned on the intended concept**, not a test that every rejected definition is linguistically false. For example, a correct “leg” headline can fail an intended “bone” comparison for a word with both meanings. E1 must preserve valid secondary correspondences as well as improve the headline. Restricted and unresolved readings remain separate from clearly wrong senses.

Recurring failures include body parts replaced by book/clothing parts, colors replaced by political meanings, verbs replaced by noun homographs, and ordinary concepts replaced by shared slang or figurative senses. Every judgment has a saved reason and source-concept identifier in `e0_display_review_*.json`. [Pair-level measurements](e0_display_metrics.json) retain the judgments used in each comparison.

### Frozen development and test roles

The active split is [e0_families_v2.json](e0_families_v2.json): **1,168 development and 544 test concept comparisons**, plus **207 development and 134 test open-vocabulary comparisons**. Cases with missing comparisons remain in their assigned partition and must not disappear from coverage reporting.

Grouping uses shared concepts, expert cognate sets, lexical identity, recorded roots, and structured related-form claims. Previously inspected concepts stay in development. Version 2 added comparison-attached claims to the lemma-snapshot evidence; it retired no additional test cases. Version 1 is preserved for audit.

This prevents leakage through recorded family links and reverse pairs. Unrecorded or differently spelled family relations can remain unknown. It is not proof of complete linguistic family separation. Test definitions remain hidden during variant development.

### Open-vocabulary review: first 25 development cases

The first checkpoint saved 25 judgments for modern meaning overlap, historical evidence, and the selected display separately: [review record](e0_open_review_001_025.json). Review read all available active glosses and etymology, with classifier predictions hidden. That checkpoint left 182 development cases to review; subsequent progress is recorded below. No open-vocabulary accuracy rate is claimed.

This pass established a source-gap control. The baseline's Swedish `hatt` entry contains clothing and pastry meanings, while the English `pileus` entry includes mushroom cap. [Swedish Wiktionary's pinned revision](https://sv.wiktionary.org/w/index.php?title=hatt&oldid=4107160), last edited before the baseline, also records the mushroom-cap sense and lists `pileus` as its English translation. The comparison is valid in a specialized sense even though the baseline target glosses omit that sense. Tests must distinguish absent dictionary evidence from absent real overlap. Translation evidence can supply information that a gloss-only comparison misses; it should remain visible separately from model scores.

The pass also includes contrasting name controls: Lesotho and the Austrian town Vils have explicit matching identities in both entries; generic surname or given-name descriptions do not establish matching identities. Removing every name would discard valid comparisons. Treating every shared name category as equivalent would retain false matches.

Other recorded cases cover direct borrowing, shared borrowing, different grammatical forms, partial compound relationships, restricted senses, and forwarding entries with unresolved underlying definitions. These are source-guided agent assessments, not independent human annotations. Missing history remains separate from the practical meaning assessment.

### Open-vocabulary checkpoint: 100 cases, 2026-10-03

Reviewed IDs 26–100 add 75 explicit judgments. The saved records are [26–60](e0_open_review_026_060.json), [61–80](e0_open_review_061_080.json), and [81–100](e0_open_review_081_100.json). Packet hashes, unique review IDs, case identities, and development roles were checked. All 100 reviewed cases belong to development; 107 remain. Test cases remain unreviewed.

The added cases establish several distinct controls for the next experiments:

- **Valid primary matches must survive.** Sowing, yew, goose, statue, criterion, and jumping have explicit shared meanings. Some also have extra senses or unrelated homographs. A rule that removes every polysemous entry would destroy useful comparisons.
- **A shared English gloss can conceal different concepts.** German instrument nut and Swedish bolt nut are different objects. Comparing the bare translated word alone can falsely align them.
- **Historical agreement does not establish current equivalence.** The sources connect several word families whose displayed meanings differ, including Danish `blive` and Norwegian `blive`, and Norwegian `ball` and Swedish `balle`. History and meaning need separate measurements.
- **An inactive sense can still become the display.** Four of the first 100 reviews flag a selected definition marked inactive in the saved source data. This is a concrete selection boundary to test. Historical subject matter and obsolete usage must also be distinguished: a historical tag alone is not proof that a term is unusable.
- **Actions, agents, and relational adjectives need grammatical context.** Saving versus saver, confirming versus confirmand, and Mauritian adjective versus person are related meanings rather than interchangeable translations.
- **Specialized uncertainty needs a source check.** The chemokine/chemerin comparison remains unresolved from its saved glosses. It must not become a positive or negative reference label merely because the terms look alike.

These controls identify methods to test; they are not word-specific corrections to the classifier. The incomplete, deliberately sampled review does not estimate whole-dataset accuracy. Its judgments remain agent assessments, with historical support and practical meaning kept separate.

**Coverage gaps are established on an independently selected basic-concept panel.** Candidate-generation testing in E4 is warranted. Dictionary coverage is strong for the most frequent subtitle tokens, but weaker in later bands, especially Danish and the generic Norwegian diagnostic. Neither finding establishes classification accuracy.

**Decision at the first checkpoints:** No methodological change was adopted. E0 remained running, with these tasks outstanding:

1. Finish reference-form handling: recorded inflections have been checked; now assess explicit alternate-spelling notation and report multiword-expression coverage separately from single lemmas.
2. Use the saved development display judgments to score E1; retain secondary matches and test concept-conditioned coverage separately from default headline quality.
3. Review the blinded open-vocabulary development packet and add the planned hard-case groups. Report agent-assisted judgments separately from published expert annotations.
4. Freeze numerical acceptance limits before E1-E4 variants. Family roles and clustered development display measurements are now saved.

## Reproduction and evidence

### Completed development review and reference handling

All **207 open-vocabulary development cases across 199 recorded family groups** now have explicit meaning, history, and display judgments. [Final summary](e0_open_summary_v2.json) includes their review-file hashes and diagnostic membership. The additional records cover IDs 101–207. Reserved test cases remain unreviewed.

The display judgments include 85 direct shared-sense matches, 78 mismatched selections, eight inactive selected senses, and six unresolved displays. The other 30 judgments distinguish restricted matches, overbroad definitions, generic name matches, grammatical differences, and documented name variants. These counts describe this balanced generated sample; they are not population accuracy estimates.

The diagnostic groups overlap: 134 cases have several active meanings, 89 have several source entries, 50 involve names, 74 involve composition or derivation, 60 involve borrowing, 29 have short forms, 26 record roots from different historical languages, and 33 are observed disjoint prefix-collision controls. There are also 24 source-supported related-form controls without a shared three-character prefix. Different root languages are a diagnostic, not proof of different origin or ancestry depth.

Three externally checked source-gap controls distinguish incomplete saved glosses from real semantic disjointness: Swedish `hatt` mushroom cap, German [reflexive `sich`](https://de.wiktionary.org/w/index.php?oldid=9720151&title=sich), and German [interrogative/relative `welcher` with `welche` forms](https://de.wiktionary.org/wiki/welcher). A fourth check confirms that an English [sex-worker noun sense](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/whore) is missing from the saved entry. This last source also records usage restrictions; recovering a meaning does not remove them. The German `sich` revision predates the baseline; the `welcher` current page was checked, but its permanent-link fetch timed out.

[Corrected reference-notation results](e0_reference_notation_v2.json) expand only explicit `nese (nase)` and `røyk (røk)` alternatives. All eight affected expected comparisons already have a candidate under at least one recorded spelling. The original 1,441/1,712 exact-spelling result remains unchanged. Together with the four recorded-inflection recoveries, these are 12 additional present comparisons under documented form handling, not newly generated data. One alternative candidate has a bad selected sense; existence alone does not establish quality. Phrases and hyphenated forms remain separately reported input limitations.

The first notation extraction incorrectly required no space before parentheses and reported zero cases. Its output is retained; the corrected extraction and explicit parser checks supersede it. The first complete open summary is also retained, followed by the version adding the last source check. These are evaluation corrections, not changes to the baseline dataset.

Numerical gates are now fixed in [E0_ACCEPTANCE.md](E0_ACCEPTANCE.md). The baseline review and procedure are complete enough for exploratory E1-E4 comparisons. Unresolved judgments and the absence of independent human review remain explicit limits. They cannot be used to claim validated expert accuracy or automatic publication approval.
